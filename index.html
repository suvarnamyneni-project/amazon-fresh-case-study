01 · Overview

A grocery store that starts with dinner

Most grocery apps are organised the way a supermarket is: aisles, categories, brands. That works when you already have a list. It works less well when the real question is "what are we eating tonight?", because then the shopper has to choose a dish somewhere else, write down the ingredients, and search for each one.

Shop by Recipe puts that whole loop inside Amazon Fresh. Customers browse recipes from well-known chefs, pick how many people they are cooking for, untick anything already in the kitchen, and send the rest to the cart in one tap.

This page walks through the flow screen by screen and explains the decisions behind each one. Every screen is also linked in the clickable prototype.

02 · My role

What I owned

I was the designer on this feature during my time at Amazon. My work covered:

  • Framing the problem and the end-to-end journey, from the Fresh storefront to the cart.
  • Information architecture for recipe discovery: shelves, search, filters and the Veg Only toggle.
  • Interaction and visual design for all seven screens, built on Amazon's existing mobile patterns.
  • The first-run explainer that shows new visitors how the feature works.
  • Building a high-fidelity, clickable prototype in Figma so the whole idea could be reviewed and demoed end to end, not just described.

I worked alongside product and engineering partners, and I used the prototype as the shared reference for walking through the flow and talking through trade-offs.

03 · Problem

The gap between "I want to cook this" and "it's in my cart"

Shoppers think in meals. The store was organised in products.

To cook a single dish from Fresh, a customer had to leave the app to find a recipe, translate it into a shopping list, then search for each ingredient one by one and guess the quantities. Every step was a chance to give up, or to finish the order somewhere else.

For customers

Too much work to go from an idea to a basket, and no help with what or how much to buy for the number of people eating.

For the business

Fresh already offered a wide range and fast delivery, but it relied on shoppers arriving with a list. A recipe gives people a reason to start a shop and fills a basket with several items in one go.

The assumptions I designed around

  • People plan meals before they buy groceries, so the store should meet them at the meal.
  • A recipe is already a shopping list; making shoppers rewrite it by hand is wasted effort.
  • How much to buy depends on how many people are eating, so serving size belongs next to the ingredients.
  • Most kitchens already hold staples like oil and spices, so shoppers need an easy way to leave those out.
04 · Goals

What the design had to do

🍳

Inspire

Help people decide what to cook, with recipes grouped the way they actually think: quick meals, healthy bites, breakfast, desserts.

🧺

Translate

Turn a recipe into a basket automatically, scaled to the serving size, while letting people skip what they already have.

🧭

Stay familiar

Fit inside the Amazon Fresh shell so it feels like part of the store, not a separate app, and reuse patterns people already know.

How I'd measure success

  • Recipe-to-cart conversion: how often viewing a recipe leads to its ingredients landing in the cart.
  • Basket completeness: whether shoppers get everything a dish needs, without missing items.
  • Repeat recipe shopping: whether people come back and shop from recipes again.
  • Time to build a cart: how quickly someone goes from choosing a dish to a ready basket.

06 · Screen by screen

Design decisions

Scroll inside each phone to see the full screen.

Amazon Fresh landing
Screen 1 · Entry point

Meet shoppers where they already are

Rather than hide the feature in a menu, I gave it the hero banner on the Fresh storefront, with chef faces that signal "cooking" before you read a word.

Two actions in one bannerThe whole banner opens the recipe homepage for people ready to browse; a small "See how it works" button serves people who want to understand it first.
A taste of recipes on the storefrontA Breakfast Recipes shelf sits below the offers, so the idea is visible even to people who scroll past the banner. Each card already has its own Shop items button.
Delivery promise up frontThe delivery address, "Delivery available Tuesday" and the 2-hour Prime Now badge stay at the top, because the value of a recipe drops if the ingredients arrive too late to cook.
How it works
Screen 2 · Onboarding

Explain the model in four pictures

Buying from a recipe is a new idea for a grocery store, so I added a short, skimmable explainer instead of a tutorial.

One line per stepBrowse recipes by top chefs, choose a serving size, modify or shop the ingredients, get delivery in 2 hours. Each step pairs a short line with an illustration, alternating left and right so the eye zig-zags down the page.
End on the rewardThe page closes with "Enjoy your meal!" rather than a feature list, keeping the focus on the outcome.
Shop by Recipe homepage
Screen 3 · Discovery

Shelves by occasion, not by cuisine

The homepage answers "what kind of meal do I want?" first. Recipes sit in horizontal shelves: Quick Cooking, Healthy Bites, Chef Special, Breakfast Recipes and Desserts.

Cards that answer the key questions at a glanceEach card shows the dish photo, chef, rating with review count, a cooking-time badge and a veg/non-veg marker, so people can compare without opening each recipe.
Veg Only as a first-class toggleFor many shoppers, vegetarian is a firm rule rather than a preference, so it sits next to Filters at the top of the page instead of inside it.
A shortcut for the decidedShop items on every card lets returning customers skip the recipe page and go straight to the basket.
WayfindingA "Shop by recipe" label replaces "Shop by category" in the Fresh tab bar, so people always know which mode of the store they are in.
Recipe search results
Screens 4–5 · Search

Search that feels like Amazon search

When someone already knows what they want, for example "chinese recipes", they search. I deliberately reused the standard Amazon results layout so nothing has to be learned.

List view for comparisonResults switch from shelves to a vertical list with a larger thumbnail, so time, rating and chef line up and are easy to scan down.
Same filters everywhereVeg Only and Filters carry over from the homepage in the same position.
Room for sponsored productsA sponsored placement sits above the results, keeping Amazon's ad inventory without breaking up the recipe list.

The Figma file has a second frame of this screen (v2) with the same layout and extra space at the bottom; both are in the prototype.

Recipe page
Screen 6 · Decision

Everything needed to commit to a dish

The recipe page is where browsing turns into buying, so I ordered it by the questions people ask before they cook: can I make this, how long will it take, and what do I need?

Confidence firstCategory, rating, an image carousel, the cooking-time badge and the chef's name and photo come before any instructions.
Serving size drives the basketA serving-size picker sits right above difficulty and prep, cooking and total time, so the quantities that follow match the number of people eating.
An ingredient list that is also a checklistA green tick means the item goes in the basket; a yellow plus means it was left out and can be added back. In the example, five of eight ingredients are ticked, and those five are exactly what show up in the cart.
One clear primary actionA full-width Shop Ingredients button closes the ingredient list. Directions, reviews with photos and "Rate this recipe" follow for people who want to read more.
Shop items
Screen 7 · Conversion

Land in the cart people already trust

Instead of building a special recipe checkout, I sent ingredients into the regular Amazon Fresh cart as real, branded products.

Real products, editableEach ingredient becomes a specific product with price, stock status, a quantity picker and Delete, so customers stay in control of brand and amount.
Clear next stepsThe subtotal, how much more is needed for free delivery, a primary "Buy Amazon Fresh Items" button and "Continue shopping on Amazon Fresh" make it easy to check out or keep adding.

08 · Outcomes

What the design enables

  • One tap from ingredients to cart. A whole recipe becomes a basket without a single manual search.
  • Clearer recipe discovery. Occasion-based shelves, search and the Veg Only toggle help people find a dish that fits the moment.
  • Fewer missed items. The ingredient checklist makes it obvious what is going in the basket and what has been left out.
  • A reusable recipe card pattern. The same card, with chef, rating, time, diet marker and Shop items, works on the storefront, the recipe homepage and search.

Just as important, the clickable prototype made the concept easy to review and demo: anyone could tap through the full journey instead of imagining it from static screens.

09 · Reflection

What I took away

Reusing patterns is a design choice, not a shortcut. The most useful decisions here were about what not to invent: the Fresh shell, the search results list and the standard cart. That left the new ideas, the shelves and the ingredient checklist, room to stand out.

The ingredient checklist does the most work. It is the one interaction that turns a recipe into a personal basket, so it is the first thing I would put in front of shoppers in a usability test.

What I would do differently. I would bring shoppers into the process earlier. I designed around reasonable assumptions about how people plan meals and shop for them, and I would rather have those assumptions challenged before the visual design gets polished. Working on this taught me that the most valuable thing a prototype does is start the right conversations.

10 · Next steps

What I'd propose next

Usability-test the checklist

Put the ingredient checklist in front of shoppers to see whether the tick and plus states read clearly, and refine them based on what I learn.

"Already have it" toggle

Make the checklist pantry-aware, so staples a shopper usually skips, like oil or salt, can start out left out of the basket.

Serving-size scaling

Update ingredient quantities and the basket live as the serving size changes, so the link between the two is visible.

These are proposed directions for the next round of design work.
